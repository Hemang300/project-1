If you're in it right now

Short, practical, no lectures.

In a cab or auto
  • Before you sit: photograph the number plate and send it to someone. Doing it visibly is itself a deterrent.
  • Share the live trip from the app. If it's a street auto, share your live location from WhatsApp instead — 8 hours, not 15 minutes.
  • Sit in the back, on the side away from the driver, with the door unlocked on your side.
  • If the route feels wrong, say out loud on a call that you're near a specific landmark. Wrong turns at low speed are the moment to get out, not the highway.
  • You don't owe anyone conversation. Not answering personal questions is normal and fine.
Walking, late or alone
  • One earphone out, or none. Hearing footsteps behind you matters more than the playlist.
  • Walk facing traffic where you can, in the middle of the footpath, away from parked vehicles and doorways.
  • If you think you're being followed, cross the road. If they cross too, go into any open shop, petrol pump, hospital or hotel lobby and tell the staff. Don't walk to your own door.
  • Shout what's happening, not just noise — "this man is following me" makes people look. "Fire" makes people come.
  • Keys between your fingers is a myth that injures you. A loud whistle, your phone's siren and distance work better.
Harassment at work
  • Write it down the same day: date, time, exact words, who else was around. A contemporaneous note carries real weight later.
  • Every workplace with 10 or more employees must have an Internal Committee under the POSH Act, 2013. Ask HR for its members in writing.
  • Fewer than 10 employees, or the complaint is against the employer? Go to the Local Committee through your District Officer, or file on the government's SHe-Box portal.
  • The complaint should normally be filed within three months, though the committee can extend that for good reason.
  • Conciliation can only happen if you want it, and it can never be a money settlement dressed up as one.
Online harassment, stalking, leaked photos
  • Screenshot everything with the URL, handle and timestamp visible, before you block. Blocking first loses the evidence.
  • Report at cybercrime.gov.in — the women-and-children section lets you report anonymously.
  • For intimate images shared without consent, also file directly with the platform under its non-consensual imagery policy, which is faster than the police route.
  • Money already lost? Call 1930 immediately. The first hour is when a transfer can still be frozen.
  • Sextortion is a crime against you, not by you. Don't pay — it never stops at one payment.
Violence at home
  • The Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005 covers physical, sexual, verbal, emotional and economic abuse — and applies to live-in relationships, not only marriage.
  • You have the right to stay in the shared household. Being thrown out is itself something a court can reverse.
  • Every district has a Protection Officer and a One Stop Centre. Call 181 and ask them to connect you.
  • Keep somewhere else: ID, bank papers, marriage documents, children's papers, a spare phone, some cash. With a friend, not in the house.
  • Get injuries examined by a doctor and keep the paper. Medical records are the evidence that lasts.

Your rights

What Indian law already gives you. Saying it out loud, at the desk or in the HR meeting, changes how things go.

Any police station, anywhere

A Zero FIR can be filed at any station regardless of where the incident happened. It is then transferred to the right one. "Not our area" is not a valid refusal.

BNSS, 2023 — registration irrespective of jurisdiction

An FIR cannot be refused

For a cognizable offence, registration is mandatory and free. If it's refused, send your complaint in writing to the Superintendent of Police, or go directly to a Magistrate. You're entitled to a free copy of the FIR.

Your statement, your terms

In sexual offence cases the statement is recorded by a woman officer, and it can be recorded at your home or another place you choose, with someone of your choice present. If you have a disability, an interpreter or special educator must be provided and the statement videographed.

Arrest timings

A woman should not ordinarily be arrested before 6am or after 6pm, and only by a woman officer. Exceptions need a magistrate's prior written permission.

Free treatment, no FIR needed first

Every hospital, government or private, must give free first aid and treatment to a survivor of sexual assault or acid attack, and inform the police itself. They cannot ask you to bring an FIR first.

No two-finger test

The Supreme Court has banned it outright and called it re-victimising. Any doctor who conducts one is committing misconduct.

You can report late

There is no time limit on reporting a sexual offence. A delayed complaint is still a valid complaint.

Every workplace with 10+ staff needs a committee

An Internal Committee, headed by a senior woman, with an external member from an NGO or legal background. Not having one is itself a violation, and HR must tell you who is on it.

Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (POSH) Act, 2013

Smaller workplaces are still covered

Under 10 employees, informal work, domestic work, or a complaint against the employer himself — go to the Local Committee through your District Officer, or file online on the government's SHe-Box portal.

Relief while the inquiry runs

You can ask for a transfer for yourself or the respondent, up to three months of paid leave over and above your normal leave, or for him to be stopped from reporting on your work. The inquiry should finish within 90 days.

Retaliation is not allowed

Being sidelined, denied a promotion, or pushed out after complaining is itself actionable. Conciliation can only happen if you ask for it, and it can never be a cash settlement.

26 weeks of paid maternity leave

For your first two children; 12 weeks after that, and 12 weeks for an adopting or commissioning mother. You cannot be dismissed for being pregnant, and workplaces with 50+ employees must provide a creche.

Maternity Benefit (Amendment) Act, 2017

Equal pay for the same work

Discriminating on pay or on recruitment for the same or similar work, on the ground of sex, is prohibited.

Code on Wages, 2019

Abuse is more than bruises

The law covers physical, sexual, verbal, emotional and economic abuse — including withholding money, taking your salary, or threatening to throw you out.

Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005

It applies to live-in relationships

You do not have to be married. Relationships "in the nature of marriage" are covered, as are mothers, sisters and daughters facing abuse from family members.

The right to stay in the house

You cannot be evicted from a shared household, whether or not you own it or your name is on the rent. A court can order him to leave instead.

Orders a magistrate can pass

Protection orders stopping contact, residence orders, monetary relief for expenses and lost earnings, custody of children, and compensation. Apply through a Protection Officer — every district has one — or a lawyer.

Cruelty and dowry are crimes

Cruelty by a husband or his relatives is a criminal offence, as is demanding dowry, giving it or taking it. Harassment over dowry is punishable regardless of whether anything was paid.

BNS s.85 (earlier IPC 498A); Dowry Prohibition Act, 1961

Streedhan is yours alone

Everything given to you before, during or after marriage — jewellery, cash, gifts — is your absolute property. Your husband and in-laws are only custodians and must return it on demand.

Maintenance

A wife, including a divorced wife who hasn't remarried, can claim maintenance from her husband. It can be claimed through the criminal court, the family court, or under the Domestic Violence Act.

BNSS s.144 (earlier CrPC s.125)

Daughters inherit equally

A daughter is a coparcener in ancestral property from birth, with the same rights and liabilities as a son. The Supreme Court confirmed in 2020 that this applies whether or not the father was alive in 2005.

Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act, 2005; Vineeta Sharma v. Rakesh Sharma

Marriage doesn't end it

Getting married does not take away your share in your parents' property. Signing a "relinquishment" at a wedding under pressure can be challenged.

Banking on your own

You can open an account, take a loan, hold property and run a business without a husband's, father's or any male relative's signature or consent. A bank asking for one is in the wrong.

Your salary is yours

Nobody in your household has a legal claim on your earnings. Being made to hand over your salary is economic abuse under the Domestic Violence Act.

Lower stamp duty

Most states charge women buyers a reduced rate of stamp duty on property registration. Worth asking about before a sale deed is drawn up in someone else's name.

Your name stays out of it

Publishing the identity of a survivor of a sexual offence is itself a crime. Media, police and online accounts cannot name you or print details that identify you.

Intimate images must come down in 24 hours

Platforms are required to remove non-consensual intimate imagery, including morphed and AI-generated images, within 24 hours of a complaint. Sharing such images is separately punishable.

IT Rules, 2021; IT Act s.66E, 67A

Report anonymously

cybercrime.gov.in has a dedicated section for crimes against women and children where you can report without giving your name. For money already lost, call 1930 within the hour.

Online stalking is stalking

Repeatedly contacting you, or monitoring your internet or messaging activity against your wishes, is a criminal offence — not "just messages".

BNS s.78 (earlier IPC 354D)

Sextortion is a crime against you

You have committed no offence by being in the images. Don't pay. Screenshot, report, and tell one person you trust.

A lawyer costs you nothing

Every woman is entitled to free legal aid through the District Legal Services Authority, whatever her income. Call 15100 or walk into the DLSA office at your district court.

Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987 — s.12(c)

Trials held in private

Sexual offence trials are conducted in camera, and as far as practicable before a woman judge. The public and press are kept out.

Your past is not on trial

Evidence of your character or previous sexual experience is not admissible on the question of consent. Questions about it can be objected to.

Compensation, separate from the verdict

Victim compensation schemes run through the Legal Services Authority, and can be awarded even where the accused is not convicted or not traced.

You must be kept informed

You're entitled to be told how the investigation is progressing, and to be heard before the accused is released on bail in serious offences.

India's criminal laws were replaced in July 2024 — section numbers changed from the IPC and CrPC to the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita and Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, but these protections carried over. This page is general information, not legal advice. For anything specific, talk to a lawyer or call 15100.

Recognise abuse

It isn't only physical

Domestic violence can happen to anyone, in any household. It can be physical, emotional, sexual, financial or psychological — and none of it is your fault.

  • Constant fear or walking on eggshells at home
  • Being cut off from family, friends or your own money
  • Insults, humiliation or constant criticism
  • Jealousy, control, or being told what to wear
  • Threats, intimidation or guilt-tripping
  • Physical harm, or the threat of it
  • Your salary or spending controlled by someone else
  • Your phone, calls or messages monitored
